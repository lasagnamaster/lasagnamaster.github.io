How scoring worksA score from 0 to 100

The deviation of each room average from 20 °C is evaluated at every step over all 72 hours. Every room has equal weight.

At 20 °C you earn 100 points. An error of 1 °C earns 75 points for that moment; 2 °C earns 50; 4 °C or more earns 0. The final score is averaged over rooms and time.

Comfort is the share of time within 19.5–20.5 °C. Energy use is shown separately and does not affect the score.

Autopilot uses the same model and power limits. It offsets current heat losses and solar gains; temperatures are never overridden.

Air is divided into cells. Walls, floors, and the roof retain the original RC nodes.

FROM COURSEWORK TO APPLICATION

One house. Two scales.

Based on the Week 3 solution: a two-storey house with zones T₁–T₄, windows, exterior walls, partitions, an inter-storey slab, ground floors, and a roof. Default ground temperature: 5 °C.

Rooms are numbered left to right: T₁–T₂ upstairs, T₃–T₄ downstairs. Controls, charts, and CSV exports use this order. The original coursework numbers are mapped to this display order; fitted parameters and physical connections are retained.

Original RC model

16 states, using the display numbering: T₁–T₄, O₁–O₄, V₁₂, V₁₃, V₂₄, V₃₄, F₃–F₄, P₁–P₂. Numerical parameters come from Week3_identified_model.npz. Each construction has its own heat capacity. For shared walls, floors, and the roof, each resistance value represents one of two equal halves.

Cᵢ dTᵢ/dt = Σⱼ (Tⱼ − Tᵢ) / Rᵢⱼ + Qᵢ

Spatial field

Each room is divided into 20 × 14 cells. Their total heat capacity equals Cᵢ from the solution. Conductances to constructions and windows are distributed over the corresponding boundaries, preserving their totals. Within a room, heat flows between adjacent cells through an adjustable conductance.

This is an additional assumption: the original measurements contain only room-average temperatures. They do not identify the spatial field or mixing rate. Walls and the roof display their RC-node temperatures; the section and pitched roof are schematic. There is no separate attic node.

Heat source and daily cycle

The heater runs continuously. Its selected power is distributed around the chosen point using a normalised Gaussian profile within one room. In the original RC model, this power enters that room's air node. In Daily cycle mode, Q = 0 in every room, while outdoor temperature follows a 24-hour sinusoid.

Keep 20 °C challenge

72 simulated hours with four independent whole-room heaters, each rated from 0 to 2.5 kW. Outdoor temperature follows a daily cycle with a cold spell on day two. The sun rises on the right at 06:00 and sets on the left at 18:00. Solar heat enters cells near windows, exterior walls, and the roof, with stronger gains on the right in the morning and on the left in the evening.

Challenge parameters are locked. To make manual control practical during the accelerated three-day run, effective air heat capacities are eight times the original values and mixing conductance is fixed at 60 W/K. Bare-house R values and construction heat capacities are retained; third-day weather modifies exterior heat exchange and adds snow insulation as described below. Manual control and autopilot use the same fixed teaching scenario.

The target and score refer to each room's average temperature. Autopilot calculates heating power from the current heat balance, limited to 0–2.5 kW. The same numerical simulation determines the temperatures and final score.

Day 3: wind, snow, and insulation

A winter storm begins at hour 48. Wind rises smoothly to 42 km/h and adds up to 6 °C of outdoor cooling; the cold front adds another 2 °C. Wind raises window conductance by up to 18% and the outer wall/roof conductance by up to 25%. Snowfall weakens solar gain. Snow builds up gradually to a 20 cm blanket and remains through the end of the run.

Accumulated snow adds resistance to the outer roof branch: at full cover its resistance is 3.5 times the bare value. With the unchanged inner half, steady roof heat transfer is then about 56% lower at the same temperature difference in calm weather. The displayed reduction describes the roof only. Walls and windows still lose heat. These prescribed values are a teaching scenario, not measured material properties. Autopilot responds to the same changing heat flows.

Numerical simulation

The app uses explicit Euler integration with Δt ≤ 0.88 min(Cᵢ / ΣGᵢⱼ), capped at 30 seconds. The challenge adds a conservative stability margin for wind-enhanced exterior heat exchange. Internal heat flows cancel in pairs. The speed slider changes playback speed only. The coursework report uses trapezoidal integration, so the numerical methods differ.

R is displayed in K/kW, C in kJ/K, and power in kW. The solver uses W, J, and seconds. The fitted parameters are effective values; they do not uniquely identify the materials or dimensions of a real house. The app works offline without external libraries.
